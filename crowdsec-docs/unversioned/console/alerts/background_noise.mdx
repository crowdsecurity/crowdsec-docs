A large share of the attacks your engines detect is not aimed at you. **Background noise** - sometimes called Internet Background Radiation - is the constant, untargeted mass scanning and bruteforcing that hits every public IP on the internet. It is real malicious traffic, but it tells you nothing about your own exposure. [More on how we qualify it](https://www.crowdsec.net/blog/background-noise-filter-available-crowdsec-console).

Noise canceling scores every alert against what the CrowdSec network sees globally, and lets you filter the noise out - so the alerts left on screen are the ones **most likely targeting you specifically**.

## Turn the noise down

Open the [filters](/u/console/alerts/search) of the Alert Explorer: **Noise canceling** is the first entry of the Source section. The panel shows the share of your current alerts that qualifies as background noise - on internet-facing infrastructure it is often the majority of them.

The slider sets how aggressive the filter is:

- **Low cancellation** keeps everything visible, including the most widespread scanning - nothing slips through unseen.
- **High cancellation** keeps only the alerts whose sources are barely seen elsewhere on the network - the ones most likely probing *your* infrastructure on purpose.

Our analysis puts the sweet spot around alerts holding **less than 50% noise**: high enough to drop the mass scanning, low enough to keep everything with a real chance of being targeted.

Like every filter, the active noise level travels in the URL and can be [saved into a view](/u/console/alerts/views) - a pinned "targeted only" view is a good default lens for daily triage.
