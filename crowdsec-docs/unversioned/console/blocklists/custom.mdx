Custom blocklists let you apply your own threat intelligence through CrowdSec. Create a private list of IPv4 addresses, IPv6 addresses, and CIDR ranges, then subscribe the Security Engines or integrations that need it.

Custom blocklists are available on the Premium and Enterprise plans.

## When to use a custom blocklist

- **Respond to an incident.** If your team confirms repeated attacks against a public login service, add the malevolent IPs to a custom blocklist.
- **Apply trusted threat intelligence.** Import IP addresses and ranges from your security team or a trusted partner.
- **Control where the policy applies.** Choose specific Security Engines or Integrations for a service-specific threat.
- **Keep protection current.** Add newly confirmed addresses, remove entries that no longer warrant blocking, or replace the contents with an updated set.

## Create a custom blocklist

Open [Custom Blocklists in the Console](https://app.crowdsec.net/blocklists/custom) and click **Create blocklist**.
Enter a **Name** and **Description** so teammates know what the list covers. Then, provide one IP address or CIDR range per line, or choose or drop a plain-text `.txt` file. Blank lines are ignored. Select **Ignore invalid entries** to skip malformed lines and keep valid ones.

![Custom Blocklists overview with the Create blocklist button highlighted](/img/console/custom-blocklists/overview.png)

![Create form with a name, description, and three valid example entries](/img/console/custom-blocklists/create.png)

:::note
Creating a list stores the entries. Subscribe an asset to put the list into use. If you create an empty list, you can add entries later.
:::

The Console supports up to 10 custom blocklists, each accepting up to 20,000 entries.

## Subscribe to a custom blocklist

Your organization’s custom blocklists are under **Blocklists → Custom**. CrowdSec’s blocklists are in the **Catalog**.

1. Open your list from **Custom** and click **Subscribe**.
2. Choose **Entire Organization**, **Security Engines**, or **Integrations**. For a targeted subscription, select the systems you want to protect.
3. Choose a remediation and click **Confirm subscription**.
4. Check **Subscribers** on the list details page to verify that the intended target appears.

![Subscription dialog showing organization, Security Engine, and integration options and the remediation selector](/img/console/custom-blocklists/subscribe.png)

For more on subscription scope and remediation, see [Blocklist Subscription](/u/console/blocklists/subscription).

## Manage a custom blocklist

Open a list from **Blocklists → Custom** to view its details.

![Custom blocklist details showing the entry count and the Subscribe, Add IPs, Download content, and Delete blocklist buttons](/img/console/custom-blocklists/details.png)

- **Find or export entries.** Use **Search IPs or CIDR ranges** to search the full list. The details page displays a limited set of rows. Use **Download content** to export the full list.
- **Add entries.** Click **Add IPs**, paste entries or choose a `.txt` file, and click **Review changes** before confirming.
- **Replace entries.** In **Add IPs**, select **Erase the existing blocklist content**, then click **Review changes**. Review the replacement count before clicking **Confirm replacement**. The new batch replaces all existing entries.
- **Remove entries.** Use the delete control beside an entry, or select several entries and confirm their removal. **Select all IPs** selects the full list, including entries outside the visible rows.
- **Rename the list or update its description.** Click the pen icon nearby the blocklist description at the top of the page.

![Replacement review showing the entry count, 30-day expiration notice, and overwrite warning](/img/console/custom-blocklists/replace.png)

Entries added or replaced through **Add IPs** expire 30 days after being added.

## Delete a custom blocklist

On the list details page, click **Delete blocklist** and review the confirmation. Deleting the list permanently removes its contents, subscriptions, and any sharing. Subscribers lose the protection provided by that list.
