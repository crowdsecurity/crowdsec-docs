---
title: My IP is flagged by CrowdSec
id: cti
description: Find out why your IP is flagged or blocked by CrowdSec, remove it from the community blocklist, or get your service classified as safe.
---

CrowdSec flags an IP when Security Engines in its network report malicious behavior from it. A flagged IP can end up in CrowdSec blocklists, which other organizations use to block traffic preemptively.

## Check your IP

Look your IP up in the [CrowdSec CTI](https://app.crowdsec.net/cti). The [IP report](/u/console/ip_reputation/ip_report) shows its reputation, the behaviors reported, and the [blocklists](/u/console/ip_reputation/ip_report#blocklists) it is currently in.

## Which case are you in?

| Situation | Who can fix it |
| --- | --- |
| One website or service blocks you, but your IP isn't in any CrowdSec blocklist | Only that site's owner: it runs CrowdSec and banned your IP locally. See [below](#a-website-running-crowdsec-blocks-me). |
| Your IP is in a CrowdSec blocklist | Ask CrowdSec to remove it: see [below](#remove-your-ip-from-the-community-blocklist). |
| You run a legitimate service that reaches many websites (crawler, monitoring, scanner, payment webhooks…) | Ask CrowdSec to classify your IP ranges as safe: see [below](#get-your-service-classified-as-safe). |

## A website running CrowdSec blocks me

CrowdSec can't lift a ban that a user decided on their own Security Engine. Contact the website's owner: they can remove the ban and allowlist your IP, as explained in [CrowdSec blocks legitimate traffic](/u/troubleshooting/false_positives).

## Remove your IP from the community blocklist

Use the [removal form](https://www.crowdsec.net/remove-ip-crowdsec-blocklist). Enter your IP: if it is in a CrowdSec blocklist, the form asks for the details CrowdSec needs to review your request.

The form only covers the blocklists CrowdSec distributes. It can't remove a ban that a CrowdSec user applied locally (see the previous section).

## Get your service classified as safe

If your service legitimately reaches many websites, CrowdSec can give your IPs a [**safe classification**](/u/cti_api/taxonomy/false_positives). The CTI then reports them with the `safe` [reputation](/u/cti_api/taxonomy/cti_object#reputation), and CrowdSec keeps them out of its blocklists.

The [safe classifications list](/u/cti_api/taxonomy/false_positives) shows the kinds of services that qualify: CDN exit nodes, search engine and AI crawlers, public DNS resolvers, compliance scanners, payment providers and webhooks, uptime monitoring…

CrowdSec needs to verify, automatically and over time, that an IP belongs to your service. Publish one of these:

- **A list of your IPs and ranges**, served over HTTPS in a machine-readable format such as JSON or CSV (no HTML or PDF). Examples: [Googlebot](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/ipranges/googlebot.json), [Bingbot](https://www.bing.com/toolbox/bingbot.json), [Fastly](https://api.fastly.com/public-ip-list).
- **Reverse DNS** on your IPs, under a domain you own, that resolves back to the same IP. Example: Apple's crawler IPs resolve to `*.applebot.apple.com`.

Then email [support@crowdsec.net](mailto:support@crowdsec.net) with what your service does, why it reaches other websites, and the URL of your list or your reverse DNS domain.

:::note
CrowdSec downloads your list regularly. Keep its URL and format stable: if they change, your IPs may stop being recognized.
:::

## If you use CrowdSec

- To stop banning an IP on your own Security Engines, add it to an [allowlist](/u/troubleshooting/false_positives#how-to-add-whitelists-or-prevent-the-security-engine-from-banning-a-given-ip).
- To tell CrowdSec an IP is legitimate, use **Report a false positive** in the Console's [alert visualizer](/u/console/alerts/visualizer), or the [feedback section](/u/console/ip_reputation/ip_report#feedback) of the IP report. The IP report also lets you say that you own the IP.
